# ADR-0093: The assigned coordinator rejects an event during planning with a reason, which releases its venues and returns its equipment in the same commit

- **Status:** Accepted; decision 5 (rejecting at any point in planning and releasing as for a cancelled event) is decided by the Product Owner (SCRUM-49 AC9, AC10, 19 Oct); decisions 2, 4 and 8 are defaults to review with the team
- **Date:** 2026-10-19
- **Story:** SCRUM-49 Confirm an event once its arrangements are in place (AC9, AC10)
- **Extends:** [ADR-0028](0028-review-and-decide.md), [ADR-0040](0040-equipment-review-outcome.md) (decision 15), [ADR-0047](0047-safety-officer-and-safety-review.md), [ADR-0051](0051-confirm-an-event.md) (decision 9), [ADR-0053](0053-flag-bookings-when-a-venue-becomes-unavailable.md)

## Context
SCRUM-49 AC9: when an essential arrangement can't be fulfilled (no suitable venue, or essential equipment unavailable), the assigned Event Coordinator can reject the event with a reason; its status becomes Rejected. It is always a manual decision, and the Event Organiser is notified. AC10: once rejected during planning, the event's venue bookings and holds are released, reserved equipment returns to the available quantity and open equipment requests are closed, "as for a cancelled event (SCRUM-61)". The story's "Event rejection clarification" says SCRUM-31 covers only the first approve-or-reject decision, before any venue or equipment request exists, so this is a separate route; and that the release rule was first a team assumption, not the customer's. ADR-0051 decision 9 left all of it for its own ticket, with a question for the customer. On 19 Oct the Product Owner confirmed AC9 and AC10 as written in SCRUM-49: the coordinator can reject an event at any point in planning, manually, with a reason; the organiser is notified; and on rejection its venue bookings and holds are released, reserved equipment returns to the available quantity and open equipment requests are closed, as for a cancelled event (SCRUM-61).

SCRUM-61 (cancelling an event) is not built yet, so there is no cancellation release to reuse. What exists: the venue module's withdrawal (SCRUM-93) and the Released status a replacement venue leaves behind (SCRUM-92, ADR-0053); the equipment module's reservations, which availability counts row by row (SCRUM-45, SCRUM-46); and `EQUIPMENT_NOT_NEEDED_STATUSES`, which already takes a rejected event's equipment off Technical Support's list (SCRUM-38) and refuses new reservations for it (SCRUM-46).

## Decision
1. **`POST /api/events/requests/<reference>/reject`, for Event Coordinators only, with `{"reason": ...}`.** The assigned coordinator rejects; another coordinator gets the usual "not yours" `404` and any other role `403`, as the other coordinator routes do (ADR-0019). Nothing rejects an event on its own (AC9). It answers `200` with the event request, now `rejected`, in `EventRequestSchema`'s shape.
2. **Planning is `approved` and `safety_review` (`PLANNING_STATUSES`).** ADR-0047 has the event Approved while its venues and equipment are arranged, then in Safety Review until it is confirmed, and the Safety Officer's Reject or Request Changes sends it back to Approved. Any other status is `409 rejection_not_allowed` with "Only an approved event or one in Safety Review can be rejected during planning.":
   - a submitted, under-review or awaiting-clarification request is rejected through SCRUM-31's decision (`POST …/decide`), which this route doesn't replace;
   - a confirmed event is past planning; stopping it is a cancellation (SCRUM-61);
   - a completed, cancelled or rejected event is already closed.
   An event in Safety Review can be rejected whether or not the Safety Officer has approved it: AC9 is about arrangements that can't be met, which the approval doesn't change.
3. **A reason is required:** missing or `null` is `400` on `reason` with "A reason is required to reject the event.", and a blank one gets the codebase's "Must not be blank.". It is trimmed. The checks run as SCRUM-31's do: the reference, the coordinator, the status, then the body, so a confirmed event is a `409` whatever the body says.
4. **The rejection is recorded as a decision.** It writes the same `decision_recorded` entry SCRUM-31 writes, `{"outcome": "rejected", "reason": …}` with the coordinator as actor, so the history keeps who, when and why (AC9), and it becomes the event's current decision on the organiser's record (SCRUM-31 AC9). The organiser is told with SCRUM-31's `event_request_rejected` notification ("Your event request was rejected."), the reason as its `detail`, after the commit (ADR-0015); nobody else is told. Reusing the kind and the notification needs no migration, and an organiser sees one meaning of "rejected" whenever it happened. The cost: the history doesn't say in so many words that the rejection came during planning; the entries before it (the approval, the venue requests) show it.
5. **Decided (SCRUM-49 AC10, confirmed by the Product Owner on 19 Oct): everything is released at once, in the rejection's commit,** as for a cancelled event. This answers ADR-0051's question 1. The status, the decision entry and every release below are saved in one commit, so a failure part-way leaves the event in planning with all its arrangements; the organiser's notice follows the commit. The release is one private helper, `_release_arrangements`, calling the two module functions below; none of them commits or knows why the event stopped, so SCRUM-61's cancellation can call the same helper.
   - **Venues** (`venue.services.release_event_arrangements`). Each active arrangement Venue Staff haven't approved (Pending, Conflicting, Rejected or Expired, `WITHDRAWABLE_REQUEST_STATUSES`) is withdrawn by the same `_withdraw_and_record` the coordinator's withdraw uses (SCRUM-93), with its `venue_arrangement_withdrawn` history entry naming the coordinator, so it leaves Venue Staff's queue. Each one that holds the venue, Approved or On Hold, moves to `released`, the status ADR-0053 added for an arrangement that no longer holds its venue, and its booking or hold is cancelled, so the venue is free for that period again (SCRUM-61 AC3, AC8). A hold, and the booking a hold becomes, is the request's own `booking` (SCRUM-75); a booking an ordinary approval made is found by its period, as `_booking_made_by` finds it for ADR-0053. Withdrawn and Released arrangements are left alone. A release writes no history entry of its own: the rejection's entry says why. `cancel_all_arrangements` isn't reused because it commits and refuses an event with an approved arrangement.
   - **Equipment** (`equipment.services.release_event_reservations`). The event's reservation rows are deleted, so their units are available for the period again (SCRUM-61 AC4); availability counts every row, and a flag on the row would need a migration and a change to every availability query. Each reservation's `equipment_reserved` history entry stays, so the record still says what was set aside.
   - **Open equipment requests are closed by the status** (SCRUM-61 AC9). A rejected event is in `EQUIPMENT_NOT_NEEDED_STATUSES`, so its lines leave Technical Support's list of requests awaiting action, no more can be reserved, and no outcome can be recorded or revised on them (SCRUM-48 AC8, ADR-0040 decision 15: `409 conflict` naming the status). The lines keep their status and last outcome: no `closed` line status was added, as it would need a migration and mean the same as the event's status.
6. **Closing the equipment requests is SCRUM-48 AC8's refusal, not new code here.** The reading of "closed" that SCRUM-61's equipment clarification gives ("so that Technical Support cannot record an outcome against an event that is no longer happening") is what ADR-0040 decision 15 already builds for every completed, cancelled or rejected event, so a rejection closes them simply by setting the status.
7. **No migration.** `rejected`, `released`, `decision_recorded`, `venue_arrangement_withdrawn` and `event_request_rejected` all exist on `main`.
8. **Default: a rejected event's decision may still be changed through SCRUM-31,** as for any rejected request. SCRUM-31 AC7's lock counts an equipment request or a venue booking request that hasn't been withdrawn or released (ADR-0084). A rejection withdraws or releases every venue request, so an event rejected during planning with no equipment request could be approved again through `/decide`, back into planning with nothing arranged; one with an equipment request stays locked. Listed under Still owed.

**The frontend contract (CONVENTIONS §14):** the new route's mock and contract tests come with the screen that uses it. SCRUM-48 AC8's integration check, an `it.todo` until now, runs in `planning.int.test.ts` on an event the coordinator rejected (nothing can cancel one yet).

## Consequences
- An event that can't go ahead no longer sits in planning holding venues and equipment: one action rejects it, frees what it held and tells the organiser why.
- Venue Staff see a released arrangement as Released (or a withdrawn one as Withdrawn) on the event's list; an approved booking they made is cancelled without a notice to them (Still owed).
- Technical Support can't record or revise an outcome on a rejected event (ADR-0040 decision 15), so its equipment record stays as it was when it was rejected.
- SCRUM-61 can reuse both release functions as they are: they don't commit, and neither knows why the event stopped.

## Still owed
- **Venue Staff aren't told** when an approved booking or a hold is released; the ACs don't ask for it. A ticket to raise if they should be.
- **Should a planning rejection be final?** After it, ADR-0084's lock no longer counts the released venues, so an event with no equipment request can be re-decided through SCRUM-31 (decision 8). A question for the team: keep that, or lock the decision once an event has been rejected during planning.
- **The organiser's history shows the rejection as a decision.** If the team wants the page to word a planning rejection differently, the entry needs a marker, which is a small change here.

## Questions for the customer
- None open here. ADR-0051's question 1 (release at once?) was answered by the Product Owner on 19 Oct (decision 5). Whether the organiser sees the Safety Officer's decision on a Confirmed event is still open (ADR-0051 decision 10) and nothing here depends on it.

## Revisit when
- SCRUM-61 lands: check that cancelling calls `_release_arrangements` and that this route still differs only in its status and wording.
- A cancelled-arrangement status or a released flag on reservations lands: use it instead of Released or deleting rows.
