# ADR-0036: A submitted request waits unassigned in the Event Coordinator Lead's queue, and only the Lead gives it to one coordinator, named by email

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-11
- **Story:** SCRUM-95 Newly submitted event requests come to the Event Coordinator Lead unassigned (Week 7 customer change #5; seven ACs, no comments)
- **Supersedes:** [ADR-0018](0018-least-loaded-coordinator-and-event-history.md)'s least-loaded assignment on submission, and its reading of SCRUM-28 AC3 as "only the assigned coordinator is told"; [ADR-0030](0030-organiser-told-about-assignment.md) decision 8's list of test names (decision 17 below)
- **Extends:** [ADR-0021](0021-coordinator-reassignment.md) (a coordinator named by email; the history's actor), [ADR-0034](0034-coordinators-see-only-their-own-events.md) (which left the queue to this story), [ADR-0015](0015-in-app-notifications.md) (one notification row per recipient, its own commit)

## Context
Since SCRUM-30 a submitted request was assigned the moment it was saved, to whichever Event Coordinator held the fewest open requests, and that coordinator was the only person told (ADR-0018). Week 7 customer change #5 replaces that with a person: an Event Coordinator Lead, who sees every request nobody is working yet and decides who works it.

SCRUM-95's ACs say a submitted request is assigned to nobody and enters the unassigned queue (AC1); only the Lead can view that queue, which shows each request's name, date, time, expected attendance and submission time (AC2); the Lead assigns a queued request to exactly one Event Coordinator, after which it leaves the queue (AC3); the Lead is notified when a request enters the queue (AC4); the assigned coordinator and the organiser are notified and the event appears in the coordinator's list (AC5); Event Coordinators cannot assign events to themselves or anyone else (AC6); and an account can be given the Lead role (AC7).

ADR-0034 already removed the coordinators' unassigned list and said the queue "is SCRUM-95's". ADR-0030 recorded SCRUM-39 AC5 (the Lead's notice) as blocked on this story.

Left open:
1. **What becomes of SCRUM-30 and SCRUM-28 AC3,** which are Done and whose ACs the customer has now changed.
2. **What becomes of the code that implemented them,** in a repo that bans dead code.
3. **Who the Lead may assign,** and how they are named, when no route lists the coordinators and user ids are never shown to other users (ADR-0021 decision 3).
4. **Which requests the Lead may assign.** ADR-0012's nine statuses include drafts, finished events, and a request that already has a coordinator.
5. **What "submission time" (AC2) is,** when `EventRequest` carries only `created_at` and `updated_at` and there is no `submitted` history kind.
6. **What a queue entry carries,** given that AC2 lists what the queue *shows* and AC3 needs the Lead to act on a row.
7. **How the assignment is audited,** when SCRUM-30 wrote its history entry with a NULL actor because the system chose.
8. **What the requests already assigned by SCRUM-30 should become.**
9. **What the existing tests of SCRUM-30 and SCRUM-28 become,** and what other records cite them.

## Decision

1. **SCRUM-30 and SCRUM-28 AC3 are superseded, not reverted (95-1).** Both stories stay **Done**: they met their ACs when they were written, were accepted, and shipped. The customer changed the requirement afterwards, which is a change forward, not a defect. No commit of either story is reverted; their behaviour is replaced in place and this record says what replaced it. Their tests are re-aimed rather than deleted (decision 17), so the suite still says what the old rule was and that it is really gone.

2. **A sixth role, `event_coordinator_lead`, internal (95-2, AC7).** It joins `Role` and `INTERNAL_ROLES`, so the Lead reaches every "internal roles" route (the event history, organisations, the venue calendar, the equipment catalogue) without any of them changing. Accounts are made the way all staff accounts are, with `flask create-staff-user` (ADR-0023); there is no promotion command, because the role is just a value on the account.

3. **The queue is `GET /api/events/requests/unassigned`, Lead only (95-3, AC2, AC6).** `@requires_role(Role.EVENT_COORDINATOR_LEAD)` is the whole of the access rule: every other role, Event Coordinators included, gets `403`. It answers in the same shape as every other role's list, with the `message` an empty list carries (ADR-0026). The path is the one ADR-0034 freed when it removed the coordinators' version, so it now resolves to this route rather than falling through to the record route as an unknown reference.

4. **One shared definition of "waiting for the Lead" (95-4).** `WAITING_FOR_LEAD_STATUS = EventRequestStatus.SUBMITTED` is read by both the queue's query and the assign guard, so the list and the action cannot drift apart: "in the queue" and "assignable" are negations of each other by construction. `_not_a_draft()` is the precedent — the codebase already keeps one definition of "past the draft stage" in one place and shares it, for the same reason. A request still needs `coordinator_id IS NULL` to be in the queue; that is the other half of the condition, and decision 10 explains why it is a filter and not a migration.

5. **A queue entry carries its `reference` as well as AC2's six fields (95-5).** The fields are the event's `name`, `proposed_date`, `start_time`, `end_time`, `expected_attendance` and `submitted_at`. AC2 enumerates what the queue *shows*; AC3 requires the Lead to act on a row, and the assign route is keyed by the reference, so a queue without it made AC3 unreachable through the API — the Lead could see a request and have no way to name it. ADR-0004 makes the reference the public identifier clients use, and it is neither a user id nor an internal row id, so including it breaks no rule. **This was found during implementation, after the queue had already shipped with the six fields**, and is worth the team seeing: a list and the action on its rows are one feature, and reading AC2 alone made the endpoint look complete when it was not. `UnassignedRequestSchema` subclasses `EventRequestSchema` and restricts the dump with `Meta.fields`, so the field definitions stay in one place and nothing else leaks out — the status, for instance, is the same for every row and says nothing.

6. **Submission time is the request's `created_at`, shown as `submitted_at` in business time (95-6).** No `submitted_at` column and no backfill: `EventRequest` carries only `created_at`/`updated_at`, there is no `submitted` event-history kind to derive an instant from, and migration 0022 was authorised for two enum values only. The known imprecision, recorded honestly: **for a request that sat as a draft before being submitted, this is when the draft was started, not when it was submitted.** For a request submitted directly the two are the same instant. The queue is ordered by `created_at` ascending then `id` ascending — the mirror of `_list_event_requests_newest_first` — so the longest wait is at the top and two requests saved in the same instant still have a stable order. A later migration adding a real `submitted_at` would fix the imprecision and would not change the API shape.

7. **The assignment is `POST /api/events/requests/<reference>/coordinator`, Lead only (95-7, AC3, AC6).** An action route like `/reassign` and `/deactivate`, answering `200` with the request and the coordinator's `full_name` and `email` — the same contact the organiser's record shows, and no user id (ADR-0021 decision 12). AC6 needs nothing of its own: `@requires_role(Role.EVENT_COORDINATOR_LEAD)` is what refuses an Event Coordinator, whether they are assigning to themselves or to a colleague, and the service never checks the caller's role because the decorator has already proved it.

8. **The coordinator is named by email: `{"coordinator_email": "..."}` (95-8).** ADR-0021 decision 3 already fixed that shape, because user ids are never shown to other users, and **no endpoint in this story lists the coordinators**, so the frontend has no id it could legitimately post. The lookup reuses SCRUM-87's `_event_coordinator_id_by_email`, so an unknown email and another role's email get the identical `400` on `coordinator_email` and the reply never says whether someone else holds the address (ADR-0021 decision 4). The payload schema is shared too: `_CoordinatorEmailSchema` is the body both routes take, with `ReassignCoordinatorSchema` and `AssignCoordinatorSchema` named after the routes that use it. The consequence is deliberate: **the Lead types an email; there is no dropdown of coordinators**, and the web app's form says so in its own docstring.

9. **A request that already has a coordinator is refused, `409 conflict` (95-9, Review Focus 1).** `ConflictError`, with `ALREADY_HAS_COORDINATOR_MESSAGE` in the idiom of SCRUM-87's `ALREADY_ASSIGNED_MESSAGE`: "This event request already has a coordinator." Nothing is written and nothing is sent. This is a *first* assignment and never a move. Moving one is a reassignment, and there are already two homes for it: the coordinator's own is SCRUM-87's `POST /requests/<reference>/reassign`, and the Lead's is SCRUM-96's. ADR-0021 decision 6 anticipated exactly this split, saying that picking up an unassigned request "is a first assignment rather than a reassignment" and belongs to a later story — this one. Building a third path that could move an event would have given the system two answers to the same question.

10. **A request must still be waiting to be assigned: a second `409`, `request_not_assignable` (95-10).** Its own `AppError` subclass, `RequestNotAssignableError`, declared in the idiom of SCRUM-87's `RequestNotReassignableError` and never reusing it: the two refusals mean different things, and a first assignment is not a reassignment, so a client should be able to tell them apart by `error_code`. The guard reads `WAITING_FOR_LEAD_STATUS` (decision 4), so it covers every status that is not `submitted` — a draft having already been answered `404` by the staff reference lookup. **What it defends against is a row the schema permits:** `coordinator_id` is nullable and independent of `status`, so `(cancelled, NULL)` and `(rejected, NULL)` are valid rows, and a coordinator-only guard would have let the Lead assign one. **No path in the code as it stands produces such a row** — nothing sets `cancelled` at all, and `rejected` is only reached by `decide_event_request`, which requires the request's own coordinator — so today the guard refuses a state no user can currently create, and the tests reach it by saving the row directly. It is kept rather than dropped because AC3 gives the Lead "a request in the queue", the queue is one status, and the two must be the same definition; leaving the status unchecked would make the assignment's rule looser than the queue's and would depend on an accident of which transitions happen to exist.

11. **The order of checks: payload `400`, reference `404`, already assigned `409`, not waiting `409`, email `400` last (95-11).** The first failure decides the answer. The body comes first because it is the caller's own and says nothing about any request. The request's own state comes next, already-assigned before not-waiting because it has its own answer and is the more specific thing to say. **The email lookup comes last on purpose:** a refused assignment never resolves an address, so the route cannot be used to discover which emails belong to Event Coordinators by watching whether a `400` on the field or a `409` on the request comes back. The cost is the mirror of ADR-0021's: a Lead who guesses a reference learns from the `409` whether a request exists and whether it has a coordinator. That is information the Lead is entitled to — the queue shows them every request they may act on — so unlike ADR-0021's "no coordinator" case there is nothing leaked to anybody who shouldn't have it.

12. **The assignment is audited through the existing `record_event_history` (95-12, asked for by the project owner).** Kind `EventHistoryKind.COORDINATOR_ASSIGNED`, `details = {"coordinator_id": <id>}`, and `actor_id` **the Lead's user id** where SCRUM-30 wrote `None` for "the system assigned". It is added to the session before the one commit that sets `coordinator_id`, so the change and its record are saved together or neither is (ADR-0018). There is no second audit path: one kind, one writer, one table. `_details_as_shown` and `HistoryEntryAsShown.actor_name` already resolved a non-null actor generically, for SCRUM-87's reassignments, so **no serialiser change was needed** and the history route shows the Lead's name with nothing added. The enum member's docstring was corrected forward: entries SCRUM-30 wrote before the Lead existed still carry a NULL actor, and the docstring now says so instead of claiming the system always chose.

13. **AC4's notice is a new kind, `request_queued_for_lead`, raised to every Event Coordinator Lead exactly once (95-13).** "A new event request is waiting in the unassigned queue." It is raised from both submission paths — a fresh submission and a submitted draft — through the existing `_raise_notifications`, so the two cannot drift apart, and it is its own commit after the request's; a failure is rolled back and logged, and the submission still succeeds (ADR-0015). Every Lead is told, not one: the queue is shared, and which Lead picks a request up is theirs to settle. **SCRUM-28 AC3's notice to every Event Coordinator is superseded** — ADR-0018 had already narrowed it to the assignee, and now no coordinator hears about a submission at all, because none of them is involved until the Lead involves them. This meets SCRUM-39 AC5, which ADR-0030 recorded as blocked on this story. `event_request_submitted` keeps its `NotificationKind` member, its message and its Postgres value, and is still produced by nothing, exactly as ADR-0018 recorded for the same reason: Postgres enums do not lose values, and rows written before SCRUM-30 still carry it and still read with their message.

14. **The WARNING on the submission path is removed (95-14).** `_warn_unassigned` logged `event_request_unassigned` at WARNING because, under ADR-0018, a request without a coordinator meant a staffing gap. Now every request starts without one, so that line fired at WARNING on every successful submission — turning a level that means "a human should look at this" into happy-path noise, and burying the real warnings. The Lead's notification (decision 13) is the mechanism AC4 actually asks for, and it goes to the people who can act. **The helper itself survives, for its one remaining caller:** `_notify_coordinator_of_answer`, where "the organiser's answer arrived and there is no coordinator to tell" is still genuinely exceptional.

15. **The superseded code is deleted (95-15).** `_least_loaded_coordinator`, `_open_request_counts` and the `OPEN_STATUSES` constant are gone, after a repo-wide grep proved no surviving caller in `app/` or `tests/`; the `func` import they needed went with them. `_notify_about_submission` went too: once submission assigned nobody, its remaining branch was unreachable, and what is left on that path is the Leads' notice, which says what it does in its own name (`_notify_leads_of_queued_request`). The repo bans dead code, SCRUM-96 is not on this branch so nothing pending needed any of it, and **all of it is recoverable from this branch's history** if the customer ever asks for a load rule again — ADR-0018 still records what the rule was and why. `_notify_about_assignment`, which the Lead's assignment and SCRUM-87's reassignment share, is untouched.

16. **Requests already assigned are left exactly as they are; there is no data migration (95-16).** A request SCRUM-30 assigned keeps its coordinator, its history entry with the NULL actor, and its place in that coordinator's list. The queue leaves it out because of the `coordinator_id IS NULL` half of its condition (decision 4), not because anything was rewritten. Migrating them into the queue would have taken work away from coordinators who are already doing it, and unassigning them is something ADR-0021 decision 8 refused for good reason.

17. **Migration 0022, with a lossy downgrade (95-17).** `revision = "0022_add_lead_role"`, `down_revision = "0019_add_review_kinds"`. It adds `event_coordinator_lead` to `user_role` and `request_queued_for_lead` to `notification_kind`, both in an autocommit block with `IF NOT EXISTS`, as 0019 does, so a failure later in the upgrade leaves it re-runnable. Postgres cannot remove an enum value, so `downgrade()` rebuilds both types and must first deal with the rows that use them: **a Lead account goes back to `event_coordinator`**, and notifications of the new kind are deleted. Migration 0006 set that precedent — it maps a row in a status the older schema does not know back to the one status it does — and the choice is the same one: `event_coordinator` is the role a Lead was promoted from and the nearest thing the older schema has. CI replays downgrade → upgrade on an empty database, so no real data is at risk there; a rollback in a live deployment would demote the Leads, and whoever runs it has to promote them again.

18. **The existing tests are re-aimed, never deleted, and some names changed (95-18).** Three consequences worth recording:
    - `tests/events/test_submit_event_request_notifies_coordinators.py` is renamed `test_submit_event_request_notifications.py`, because nobody is notified of a coordinator on that path any more. Its tests became `test_neither_coordinator_is_told_a_request_is_theirs` and `test_the_organiser_is_not_told_a_coordinator_was_assigned` (from `test_the_assigned_coordinator_gets_one_assignment_notification`), plus the new Lead cases. `test_a_submitted_draft_goes_to_the_least_loaded_coordinator_with_history_and_a_notification` became `test_a_submitted_draft_waits_unassigned_with_no_history_or_notice` and `test_a_submitted_draft_tells_every_lead_it_is_waiting`, and `test_with_no_event_coordinator_the_request_is_saved_unassigned_and_logged` was folded into `test_a_submitted_request_is_saved_unassigned_and_only_the_lead_is_told`. **So [ADR-0030](0030-organiser-told-about-assignment.md) decision 8 now cites a module and two test names that no longer exist.** ADRs here are superseded, never rewritten — ADR-0021's own header lists four later records that amended it — so ADR-0030 is left as written and the new names are recorded here instead. SCRUM-39's markers moved with the tests, so `pytest -m scrum_39` still runs its evidence.
    - **One parametrized table was deleted with the code it covered:** `test_only_open_statuses_count_towards_a_coordinators_load` (9 cases) tested `OPEN_STATUSES`, which no longer exists, and a test of a deleted constant is not a regression guard. The question it is reasonable to still ask — "is the load rule really gone?" — is kept by a re-aimed 5-case table, `test_a_coordinators_load_does_not_decide_who_gets_it`, which arranges the same loads ADR-0018 chose by and asserts that no arrangement assigns anybody.
    - **One test was collapsed into another** because, once re-aimed, its assertions were a strict subset of a sibling's in the same file (the two listed above, both ending at "saved unassigned").

19. **[ADR-0031](0031-end-to-end-journey-through-the-test-client.md)'s rule was breached, temporarily, and the breach is closed (95-19).** ADR-0031 requires every call in the end-to-end journey to go through the real routes. Mid-branch, before the assign route existed, the journey used a fixture named `lead_assigns_outside_the_api` that fabricated the assignment by calling the service directly — named and documented so the breach could not be mistaken for the design. **The fixture was removed in the same branch, in the commit that landed the route.** The journey now signs a Lead in as an `Actor` like every other user, reads the queue over HTTP, posts the assignment to the real endpoint, and checks that an Event Coordinator gets `403` from both routes. ADR-0031's rule stands unamended.

## Consequences
- An organiser's request is answered `201` and then waits: until a Lead acts, it has no coordinator, the organiser's record shows `"coordinator": null`, and nobody is working it. How long that is is now a staffing question rather than a property of the code.
- A submission raises one notification per Lead instead of two notifications (the coordinator's and the organiser's). With no Lead account, nobody is told at all and nothing is logged — the request still sits in the queue, where the first Lead to be created will find it.
- The assignment is where three things that used to happen on submission now happen: the history entry, the coordinator's `coordinator_assigned` notice and the organiser's `request_assigned` notice (AC5). A successful assignment makes three commits, as a reassignment does.
- The event history can now show a named actor on a `coordinator_assigned` entry. Entries from before this story still show none, so the history distinguishes "the system chose" from "the Lead chose" by whether the actor is there.
- `pytest -m scrum_95` runs this story's tests. The markers `scrum_28`, `scrum_30` and `scrum_39` still run their stories' guards, which now assert the new behaviour.
- **The Lead has a queue but no detail view.** `GET /api/events/requests/<reference>` still allows only the organiser and the request's own coordinator, so a Lead sees the seven fields of a queue row and nothing more — not the notes, the requirements or the equipment. ADR-0034 expected "the Lead's own view of an unassigned request" from this story; AC2 names a queue and AC3 an assignment, neither of which needs a detail view, so it is deferred (see below).
- The web app routes a Lead to the queue page on sign-in (`ROLE_HOME`), and assignment is a typed email field with the backend's `400` shown on it. There is deliberately no coordinator picker; adding one means adding an endpoint that lists coordinators, which would also let the payload carry an id and so reopen decision 8.
- `frontend/src/integration/lead.int.test.ts` holds the queue's contract as seven `it.todo` entries, not executed tests: the integration setup cannot yet create a staff account (ADR-0023 makes that a command) or seed a submitted request. The mock handlers stand in meanwhile, so **the real backend contract is pinned only by the backend's own tests and the end-to-end journey.**
- Rolling back migration 0022 demotes every Lead to `event_coordinator` and deletes their queue notifications.

## Deferred to SCRUM-96
SCRUM-95's ACs are all met. These belong to the next story, which the plan names as their owner:
- **The Lead's oversight view of every active event with its coordinator** (SCRUM-24 AC3, which ADR-0034 recorded as blocked on SCRUM-95/96).
- **Reassignment between coordinators by the Lead,** and with it SCRUM-39 AC6 (telling the coordinator an event was moved away from them), which ADR-0030 recorded as blocked for the same reason. Decision 9 keeps the door open: the Lead's assign route refuses an assigned request today, so SCRUM-96 can decide whether the Lead reassigns through SCRUM-87's route or a route of their own.
- **The Lead's detail view of a queued request,** if the customer wants one.

## Revisit when
- The customer asks for the load to be taken into account again — a suggested coordinator, or a warning that someone is holding a lot. ADR-0018 records the rule and decision 15 says where the code is.
- A route lists the Event Coordinators. The payload could then carry an id, the web app could offer a picker, and decision 8 should be reopened (ADR-0021 decision 3 would have to be reopened with it).
- A real `submitted_at` column is added. The queue's ordering and its `submitted_at` field would then mean what they say for a submitted draft too (decision 6).
- A status transition is added that can leave a request unassigned — a cancellation, in particular. Decision 10's guard starts refusing rows that users can really create, and the queue's condition should be re-read at the same time.
- Several Leads are in use and one request is assigned twice at once. Last write wins and both are in the history, as for a reassignment (ADR-0021 decision 15); a row lock is the answer if that stops being acceptable.
- The integration suite can seed staff accounts and submitted requests: the seven todos in `lead.int.test.ts` become real checks of the contract.
