# ADR-0108: The organiser is told when a venue sends their confirmed event back and how it resolves, a replacement keeps the flagged booking's period, and "available again" names the venue and the event

- **Status:** Accepted. Telling the organiser, locking the replacement's period and the "available again" wording follow the Product Owner's decisions of 25 Oct. Naming the category and telling the organiser how the return resolves, once per event, follow the decisions of 26 Oct, which the Product Owner gave as final. The calls the criteria leave open (the organiser's kinds and wording in decisions 1, 10 and 11, and decisions 3, 6, 7, 9 and 12) were left to the team and decided here; the team may still revisit them. Decision 11 is extended by [ADR-0111](0111-replacement-approved-after-available-again-tells-the-organiser.md): a replacement approved after the venue is available again tells the organiser too, reading the categories from the history
- **Date:** 2026-10-26
- **Story:** SCRUM-92 Flag bookings affected when a venue becomes unavailable (AC3, AC6, AC8, AC9, AC10; "Changed after review", 25 and 26 Oct)
- **Extends:** [ADR-0053](0053-flag-bookings-when-a-venue-becomes-unavailable.md), [ADR-0088](0088-replacing-a-flagged-venue-in-the-web-app.md), [ADR-0100](0100-on-hold-same-venue-and-available-again.md) (supersedes its decision 8, the wording, and the "one notice per arrangement" part of decision 7)

## Context
On 25 Oct the Product Owner changed three of SCRUM-92's criteria:

- **AC3:** "The Event Organiser is notified when a Confirmed event goes back to Approved because a venue became unavailable", naming the event and the venue (customer: Week 9, A13). Only the assigned coordinator was told before (ADR-0053 decision 9).
- **AC6:** "The replacement is locked to the flagged booking's date and times, so it cannot be used to move the event." The replacement used to take any period (ADR-0053 decision 10), and the web app filled the form from the flagged arrangement but let the coordinator change it (ADR-0088 decision 4).
- **AC8:** "Available again" is sent only when nothing else still makes the venue unavailable for the booking, and names the event: "<venue> is available again for <event>. Your original booking stands.", adding that the event still needs Safety Review and confirmation when it went back from Confirmed to Approved. ADR-0100 already waited for the arrangement's last flag; its detail named the venue, the arrangement's purpose and its period.

On 26 Oct, after this ADR's first version, the Product Owner extended AC3 and added two criteria:

- **AC3** names the unavailability category too: "the Organiser sees the flag and its reason on the event record anyway (SCRUM-24)".
- **AC9:** "Given the event went back from Confirmed to Approved under the third criterion, Then the Event Organiser is also notified when the venue is available again (eighth criterion) and when a replacement venue is approved (seventh criterion), naming the event and the venue or venues." The reason: "Registered Attendees are already told about an approved replacement (SCRUM-62), so the Organiser should not hear it last."
- **AC10:** "The Event Organiser's notices under this story go once per event, to the Organiser who submitted the event."

The two new criteria move the old ninth and tenth (deactivation flags the same way; a replacement withdraws the safety approval) to eleventh and twelfth. Code and tests written before 26 Oct still call those AC9 and AC10; they are left as they are.

## Decision
1. **AC3: the organiser who submitted the request is told, once per event, after the flags' commit.** `record_alternative_venue_needed` now returns whether it sent the event back to Approved; the venue module keeps that on each notice and, after the commit (ADR-0015), calls `notify_organiser_venue_unavailable` for each event sent back. When one block flags two of an event's arrangements, only the first finds the event Confirmed, so the organiser hears once. Only the submitting organiser, as for the other decisions on their request (ADR-0028) and confirmation (ADR-0051). The notice reuses the `alternative_venue_needed` kind, whose message, "A venue booked for your event is unavailable. The event needs an alternative venue.", is true for the organiser too. The detail names the venue, the event and the category (26 Oct) and says what happened to the status: `Main Hall became unavailable for Partner Summit (equipment failure), so the event has gone back from Confirmed to Approved for its venue to be reviewed. It has not been cancelled.` Venue Staff's note stays out of every organiser notice, as it stays out of the organiser's history (ADR-0053 decision 7); the record shows it (SCRUM-24).
2. **AC6: a replacement for a booking whose venue became unavailable keeps that booking's date and start and end time.** The backend compares the request's `starts_at` and `ends_at` with the flagged arrangement's as instants (ADR-0009), so the same period written in UTC or with no offset is accepted, and an overnight booking keeps its end date too. Another period is a `400` on each time field it moves, with the period in business time: "A replacement keeps the date and times of the booking it replaces: 2026-11-01 09:00 to 2026-11-01 12:00."
3. **The period is checked after the same-venue rule,** so a replacement at the venue it replaces is refused on `venue_id` alone, whatever its period (ADR-0100 decision 5), and fixing that shows any period refusal next.
4. **The lock applies only while the arrangement needs an alternative venue** (`needs_alternative_venue`), so one flagged both ways is locked too. A replacement for an arrangement flagged only after a change keeps ADR-0100 decision 5: it may stay at its venue for a new period, because a time change is what flagged it and a replacement is how that flag clears (Product Owner, 25 Oct, 6.4).
5. **The web app prefills and locks the period.** "Find another venue" already opens the search set to the flagged booking's date and times, and "Request this venue" already fills the form from it (ADR-0088 decisions 3 and 4). While the arrangement replaced needs an alternative venue, the form's Starts and Ends are read-only and say "Fixed to the booking it replaces. In Singapore time." They are still sent, so the backend checks them. While the event's list doesn't hold the arrangement, or it is flagged only after a change, the period stays open. A refusal shows under each time field, and the event's list is read again after any answer, a refusal included, so the form sees a change it was refused for. The booking mock refuses another period the same way.
6. **AC8: the "available again" detail names the venue and the event,** `Main Hall is available again for Partner Summit. Your original booking stands.`, and the kind's message is now "A venue that was unavailable for your event is available again." The events module writes the detail, since it holds the event's name and history; the venue module passes the venue's name. "It still needs Safety Review and confirmation." is added while the event is **sent back by a venue** (decision 9). That is read for the event, not the one arrangement, because the sentence is about the event: one venue's flag may have sent it back and another's be the one clearing.
7. **One "available again" notice per event, however many of its arrangements at the venue are freed.** The detail no longer names an arrangement or its period (the Product Owner's wording names only the venue and the event), so a second notice would say the same. The arrangement left with no flag is still found as ADR-0100 decision 7 finds it.
8. **"Only when nothing else still covers the booking" stays as built** (ADR-0100 decision 7): an arrangement that another block, or the deactivation, still flags gets no notice. Tests pin two overlapping blocks and a block removed while the venue is still deactivated.
9. **"The event went back from Confirmed to Approved under the third criterion" is read from the event's history** (`_sent_back_by_a_venue`). Each flag's `alternative_venue_needed` entry records whether it sent the event back (`returned_to_planning`). Of those entries and the event's confirmations (`event_confirmed`), the newest decides: a return the coordinator has since confirmed past no longer counts. So an event confirmed again, and later back in Approved for another reason (a significant change, say), is not told it "still needs Safety Review" by a later block's removal, and its organiser hears nothing of it. AC8's sentence, and both AC9 notices, use this one check.
10. **AC9: the organiser of an event sent back is told when the venue is available again,** under AC8's condition and with AC8's kind: `venue_available_again`, whose message fits them too. The detail names the venue, the event and the lifted block's or deactivation's category: `Main Hall is available again for Partner Summit after being unavailable (maintenance). Your original booking stands. The event still needs Safety Review and confirmation.` An event the venue never sent back tells its organiser nothing, since they were never told of the flag.
11. **AC9: the organiser of an event sent back is told when a replacement venue is approved,** with a new kind, `replacement_venue_approved`: "A replacement venue was approved for your event." No existing kind fits: `venue_booking_approved` says "Your venue booking request was approved.", and the organiser raised no request. Migration 0047 adds it, revising 0046 from PR #143 (SCRUM-33), which merges first. The detail names the event, both venues and each category that flagged the original, once, in flag order: `Auditorium became unavailable for Partner Summit (maintenance), and Breakout room has been approved in its place. The event still needs Safety Review and confirmation.` It is sent after the decision's commit, only for a replacement of an arrangement whose venue became unavailable (it keeps its unavailability flags once released). A replacement for an arrangement flagged only after a change is SCRUM-33's and tells the organiser nothing, even in an event a venue sent back; so does a rejected replacement or an ordinary request.
12. **AC10: "once per event" means one notice for each event per happening:** one when a block or deactivation sends it back, one when a block's removal or a reactivation frees its arrangements, and one for each replacement approved, since each approval is its own decision. Every organiser notice goes to `organiser_id`, the Organiser who submitted the event, not to the other organisers of its client organisation.

## Consequences
- An organiser of an event sent back may hear about one venue three times: when the event goes back, when the venue is free again or a replacement is approved, and when the coordinator confirms it again (ADR-0051).
- A coordinator with two arrangements at one venue hears once when the venue is free again.
- The replacement can no longer move an event; moving it is a change request (SCRUM-56, SCRUM-57).
- This branch can't be upgraded on its own until PR #143's 0046 is on `main`; its migration test writes out the value 0046 adds.
- The mock backend has nothing to mirror for the organiser's notices: the notifications mock serves only seeded notices (ADR-0100 decision 9). The integration tests read the real ones.

## Revisit when
- An arrangement is raised at a venue during a block that already covers it: the block didn't flag it, so lifting another, later block over it would send "available again" while the first still covers it. Flagging a request raised into a block (or refusing it) would close this; it is outside SCRUM-92's criteria, which flag what a new block cuts into.
- Another migration takes 0047's place before this merges: re-point `down_revision` and add its values to the migration's list.
- SCRUM-59 lets Venue Staff reconfirm a booking: decide whether a change-flagged replacement still takes a new period.
