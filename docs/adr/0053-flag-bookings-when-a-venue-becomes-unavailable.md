# ADR-0053: A block or deactivation flags the arrangements it affects "Needs Alternative Venue", and an approved replacement releases them

- **Status:** Accepted for the flag, the notice and the replacement (SCRUM-92 AC1-AC10); decisions 4, 5, 8, 9 and 12 are defaults pending the customer; flagging tentative holds is deferred to SCRUM-75 (PR #93)
- **Date:** 2026-10-18
- **Stories:** SCRUM-92 Flag bookings affected when a venue becomes unavailable; SCRUM-49 AC6

## Context
SCRUM-34 lets Venue Staff block a venue for a period and shows them the confirmed bookings the block cuts into, but nothing happens to those bookings. The customer's Week 7 change #2 asks for more: the booking is marked "Needs Alternative Venue" with the reason and period, a Confirmed event goes back to planning, the coordinator is told, can raise a replacement linked to the flagged booking, and approving the replacement releases the original and withdraws the safety approval. Removing the unavailability clears the flag; deactivating a venue flags its future bookings the same way. SCRUM-49 AC6 refuses confirmation while a booking is flagged. The ACs leave open how the flag is stored, what the reason looks like next to SCRUM-34's free-text reason, and what happens at the edges.

## Decision
1. **The flag sits beside the arrangement's status, not in it.** A new table, `alternative_venue_flags`, holds one row per cause: the arrangement (`booking_request_id`), the block (`venue_block_id`, `null` for a deactivation), the `category`, the `reason` note and the period (`starts_at`, `ends_at`, `null` for a deactivation). An arrangement "needs an alternative venue" while it has a flag and is still live. Keeping the status means the original booking stands untouched when the flag clears (AC8), and two overlapping blocks each leave their own flag, so removing one leaves the other's.
2. **What gets flagged is a live arrangement:** Pending, Conflicting (a Pending request that clashes, ADR-0045) or Approved, which is the confirmed booking (`FLAGGABLE_REQUEST_STATUSES`). A Rejected, Withdrawn or Released one has nothing left to lose. Its flags stay as the record of why, but it no longer needs anything.
3. **"Overlapping" includes setup and turnaround.** An arrangement holds its venue from its start minus setup to its end plus turnaround (ADR-0011), the rule a block's conflicting bookings already use (ADR-0016). A block that only touches that period flags nothing.
4. **Default: the reason is a new `category` with SCRUM-34's `reason` as the note.** `category` is one of `maintenance`, `equipment_failure`, `renovation`, `safety_concern`, `other` (AC2), a Postgres enum `unavailability_category`, required on every new block. The existing `reason` keeps its name and rules (required, not blank, trimmed, at most 500 characters) and serves as the note, so SCRUM-34's API and rows don't change. Blocks saved before this become `other`. Every block counts as unavailability, including one for internal use (SCRUM-34's title), since a block is the only way to mark a venue unavailable.
5. **Default: deactivating a venue takes the same `category` and `reason`** (`POST /api/venues/<id>/deactivate` now has a body), because AC9 applies "the same flagging", and a flag has a reason. Every live arrangement at the venue that hasn't ended is flagged from the moment of deactivation, with no end. Reactivating deletes those flags; a block's flags stay until the block is removed. Deactivating a venue that is already inactive does nothing, so nothing is flagged twice.
6. **The flag, its history entry and the event's status change are saved with the block or deactivation, in one commit; the coordinator is told afterwards** (ADR-0015). A lost notice loses only itself and is logged.
7. **AC3:** only a Confirmed event goes back to Approved. An event in Safety Review stays there, as AC3 says for any event not yet Confirmed; the confirmation and Safety Review gates hold it back instead (decision 13). Each flag writes an `alternative_venue_needed` history entry with no actor (the system did it), naming the arrangement, venue, category, note, period and whether the event went back to planning. The entry is internal-only: the note is Venue Staff's, and the calendar already hides block reasons from everyone but them.
8. **Default: clearing a flag doesn't put a Confirmed event back to Confirmed.** It stays Approved; the coordinator takes it through Safety Review and confirmation again. The change review AC3 asks for has to happen somewhere, and restoring a status the system took away would confirm an event without the coordinator, which SCRUM-49 AC3 forbids.
9. **AC5: the event's assigned coordinator is told, once per flag,** with the new `alternative_venue_needed` notification kind. The `detail` names the venue, the period in business time, the category and the note: `Main Hall is unavailable from 2026-11-01 10:00 to 2026-11-01 11:00 (equipment failure): Lift broken.` A deactivation reads `… from 2026-10-20 12:00 until it is reactivated (renovation): …`. It goes to the coordinator assigned now, not whoever raised the request. With nobody assigned nobody is told, and that is logged. Default: no notice when a flag clears, and Venue Staff, who caused it, are not notified.
10. **AC6: a replacement is an ordinary booking request with `replaces_request_id`.** It must name an arrangement of the same event that needs an alternative venue; otherwise it is a `400` on that field, worded "Venue arrangement not found." (another event's arrangement is not discoverable, ADR-0035 §6) or "This venue arrangement does not need an alternative venue.". A replacement may be raised while the event is Approved or in Safety Review (decision 7 leaves a flagged event there); an ordinary request still needs Approved. Venue search already takes any period, so "open venue search for the event's original period" needs nothing new from the backend.
11. **AC7: approving the replacement releases the original** in the same commit: it moves to a new `released` status, which retires it like `withdrawn` does (it no longer counts as one of the event's venues), and its confirmed booking is cancelled. The booking is found as the event's booking at that venue for exactly that period, since a booking has no link to its request on `main`. If the original has already stopped holding its venue, say to an earlier replacement, it is left as it is. The decision's history entry records `replaces_request_id`.
12. **Default: a replacement approved after the flag cleared still releases the original.** The coordinator asked to replace it; to keep the original once its venue is free again, they withdraw the replacement first.
13. **AC10 and SCRUM-49 AC6.** Approving a replacement for an event with a safety approval writes a `safety_approval_withdrawn` history entry (actor: the Venue Staff user), which joins `SAFETY_CHECK_KINDS`, so `has_safety_approval` reads it as no approval. Confirmation (SCRUM-49) and preparation (SCRUM-84/85, which read `has_safety_approval`) need a new check. An event in Safety Review goes back to Approved so the coordinator can send it again. Confirmation and the Safety Review gate share `_venue_gaps` (ADR-0051), which now names every flagged active arrangement: `Venue arrangement 12 needs an alternative venue.`
14. **Migration `0039_flag_alternative_venues`** adds the type, the column, the table, `replaces_request_id`, `released` and the three kinds. Its downgrade deletes the new rows, turns Released arrangements into Withdrawn ones and rebuilds each enum with the values on `main`. It revises 0037, main's head when it was rebased; whichever open migration merges later re-points its `down_revision` and adds its own values to the rebuilt lists.

### Deferred
- **Tentative holds (SCRUM-75, PR #93).** An On Hold arrangement isn't on `main`, so it is not flagged and its tentative booking is not released. Once #93 merges, `on_hold` joins `FLAGGABLE_REQUEST_STATUSES`, and releasing an On Hold original also releases its hold.

## Questions for the customer
1. Should a block for internal use flag bookings too, or only maintenance-type unavailability? (We flag every block.)
2. When the unavailability is removed, should an event that went from Confirmed back to Approved be confirmed again automatically, or go through Safety Review and confirmation again? (We do the latter.)
3. Should the coordinator be told when the venue becomes available again and the flag clears?
4. Should Venue Staff have to give a reason when they deactivate a venue? (We require one, so the coordinator can be told why.)

## Consequences
- The coordinator's list of booking requests shows `needs_alternative_venue`, each `unavailability` (category, note, period) and `replaces_request_id`, and Venue Staff's queue shows the same, so the frontend's types and mocks change with this.
- `block_venue` and `deactivate_venue` now write to the events module (history, status) and the notifications module, through their services, as raising a request already does.
- A booking request has a second retired status, `released`, so anything counting an event's venues ignores it.

## Revisit when
- PR #93 (SCRUM-75) merges: flag On Hold arrangements and release their holds.
- A booking gets a link to its request (PR #93 adds `booking_id`): find the booking to cancel by it, not by period.
- The customer answers the questions above (decisions 4, 5, 8, 9, 12).
