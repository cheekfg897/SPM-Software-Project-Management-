# ADR-0047: The Safety Officer is an internal role, and the assigned coordinator sends a settled, approved event to Safety Review

- **Status:** Accepted (parts A and B of SCRUM-97; SCRUM-98)
- **Date:** 2026-10-16, amended 2026-10-17 for SCRUM-98
- **Stories:** SCRUM-97 Submit an event for operational safety check; SCRUM-98 Record the operational safety check outcome; the list is also SCRUM-24 AC6 (See the events my role is involved in)

## Context
Customer change #6 adds an Operational Safety Check. SCRUM-97 adds the Safety Officer role and a Safety Review status: an event goes there once every active venue arrangement is Approved and every equipment request has an outcome from Technical Support (AC1), with equipment optional (AC2). SCRUM-98 records the Safety Officer's outcome and SCRUM-49 lets the coordinator confirm once venue, equipment and safety are all in. SCRUM-22's status list is to be reconciled with SCRUM-31 (Approved) and SCRUM-68.

The ACs say the event "moves to" Safety Review but not who or what moves it, and leave open where it sits in the lifecycle, what happens to an event with no venue, and how the role is provisioned. Decisions 1-9 cover part A: the role, the status and the transition. Decisions 10-14 cover part B: the Safety Officer's list, what it shows for each event, and their notification.

## Decision
1. **The Safety Officer is an internal staff role**, `Role.SAFETY_OFFICER = "safety_officer"`, in `INTERNAL_ROLES`. An account gets it the way every staff account does: `flask create-staff-user --role safety_officer` (ADR-0023). No new mechanism (AC7). Like every internal role, it is let into the routes open to all internal staff: the event history (internal entries included), equipment types, venue calendars, and creating client organisations (ADR-0013). The reads help part B; creating organisations goes beyond the story.
2. **`safety_review` sits between `approved` and `confirmed`.** The lifecycle is now: draft → submitted → under review (↔ awaiting clarification) → approved → **safety review** → confirmed → completed, with rejected and cancelled as exits. That is ten statuses: SCRUM-22's seven plus `approved` (SCRUM-31), `awaiting_clarification` (SCRUM-32) and `safety_review`. It is an active status, so the Lead lists it and can reassign the event (`ACTIVE_STATUSES`, ADR-0041).
3. **The assigned coordinator sends the event, with `POST /api/events/requests/<reference>/safety-review`.** The story is titled "Submit an event", and the move needs an actor for the event history. Moving it automatically would mean venue decisions (venue module) and equipment outcomes each triggering a status change in the events module. Another coordinator gets the usual "not yours" `404` and any other role `403` (ADR-0019).
4. **Only an `approved` event can be sent.** Any other status is `409 safety_review_not_allowed` with `NOT_APPROVED_FOR_SAFETY_REVIEW_MESSAGE`.
5. **The gate, checked through public service functions:**
   - Venue: `venue.services.count_active_arrangements` and the new `list_unapproved_arrangements`. Withdrawn arrangements are retired and ignored; Pending and Rejected ones are active and hold the event back (AC1, Rejected venue clarification). There is no On Hold or Cancelled arrangement status yet, and the code needs no change when they arrive: anything active and not Approved holds the event back, anything retired is ignored.
   - Equipment: `lines_without_outcome` (ADR-0040). No equipment requests means nothing is outstanding, so the event passes (AC2).
   - A refusal is `409 safety_review_not_allowed` with `SAFETY_REVIEW_NOT_READY_MESSAGE` and `details` naming each gap, under `venue_arrangements` and `equipment`, so the coordinator knows what to chase.
6. **An event with no venue arranged is held back.** "Every active arrangement is Approved" is true of none, but the story's premise is that "the venue is confirmed", and a safety check without a venue has nothing to assess.
7. **The move is recorded** as a `safety_review_requested` history entry with the coordinator as actor and no details, in the same commit as the status, like `review_started` (ADR-0028). The organiser can see it, as they see the status.
8. **Migration `0029_add_safety_review`** adds `safety_officer`, `safety_review` and `safety_review_requested` outside the transaction. Downgrade is lossy: the entries are deleted, an event in Safety Review returns to `approved`, and a Safety Officer becomes an `attendee`, the one role with no staff access, before each type is rebuilt.
9. **Defaults to review (not agreed with the team):** decision 1's internal-route access (in particular creating client organisations), decision 3 (the coordinator sends it, not an automatic move), decision 6 (no venue means not ready), decision 8's downgrade of a Safety Officer to `attendee`.
10. **The list is `GET /api/events/requests/awaiting-safety-review`, for Safety Officers only** (AC5). Any other role gets `403` from the role check. It is one list for all Safety Officers, as SCRUM-38's is for Technical Support (ADR-0022), so there is no per-event "not yours" `404`. It holds every event in `safety_review`, ordered by proposed date, then start time, then as submitted. Past dates are not dropped: an event stays in review until SCRUM-98 decides it. It is also SCRUM-24 AC6's list (the Safety Officer's, which [ADR-0034](0034-coordinators-see-only-their-own-events.md) left blocked by this story), so like every role's list it shows each event's status (SCRUM-24 AC8) and, when empty, a `message` saying so (AC11).
11. **What each event shows** (AC6). The event: name, date, times, status, expected attendance and accessibility needs. Each active venue arrangement: the venue's name, capacity, supported room layouts, facilities and accessibility features, and the period it is booked for. Each equipment request: the type's name, quantity and notes, with Technical Support's outcome (status, quantity provided, note). No ids. "Room layout" is the layouts the venue supports: the system holds no layout for the event, and an arrangement's own layout is optional and normally empty (SCRUM-42). The venue name and period tell the venues of a multi-venue event apart, and the outcome is what the Safety Officer reviews after, so both go beyond the AC's list.
12. **Reads go through public service functions.** The venue module gains `list_arranged_venues`, built on `list_active_arrangements`, so a Withdrawn arrangement is left out. The events module reads its own equipment lines and gets the type names from `equipment.services.list_equipment_types`.
13. **Safety Officers are notified with a new `safety_review_requested` notification kind** (AC4): every Safety Officer, once, and nobody else. It is raised after the move is saved, in a second write like the other notices (ADR-0015): if it can't be saved only the notice is lost and a warning is logged, the event is still in Safety Review. A refused submission notifies no one. The organiser is not told: the story asks for the Safety Officer only. `notification_kind` is a Postgres enum, so migration `0030_add_safety_review_notification_kind` adds the value outside the transaction; its downgrade deletes those notifications and rebuilds the type, like 0019's.
14. **Defaults to review (not agreed with the team), part B:** decision 10's ordering tie-breakers and keeping past-dated events listed, and decision 11's extra fields (venue name and period, equipment outcomes).

SCRUM-98 records the Safety Officer's outcome. Decisions 15-21 cover it.

15. **`POST /api/events/requests/<reference>/safety-outcome`, for Safety Officers only** (SCRUM-98 AC9). Any other role gets `403`. Like the list, there is no per-event "not yours" `404`; an unknown reference or a draft is `404`. The body is `outcome` (`approved`, `rejected` or `changes_requested`), `checklist` and `reason`.
16. **The checklist is seven required notes**, one per AC2 item: `attendance_vs_capacity`, `venue_layout`, `emergency_access`, `accessibility`, `equipment_placement`, `crowd_movement`, `venue_restrictions`. A missing or blank note is a `400` naming it, with every outcome, since AC2 says "each with a note". The items the system holds no data for are notes like the rest (AC3). Notes and the reason are trimmed.
17. **Where each outcome leaves the event.** "Planning" is `approved`: that is where venues and equipment are arranged, and where `submit_for_safety_review` sends the event from. Reject and Request Changes both move it back there (AC6), never to `rejected`. Approve leaves it in `safety_review` for the coordinator to confirm (SCRUM-49, see Still owed), so there is no new status.
18. **An approval is read from the history, by `has_safety_approval(event_id)`**, for SCRUM-49 and SCRUM-84/85. The newest `safety_review_requested` or `safety_outcome_recorded` entry decides: it is an approval only if it is an outcome entry whose `outcome` is `approved`. An approval therefore lasts through confirmation and preparation, and is void once the event is sent to Safety Review again. An approved event is left out of the Safety Officer's list (decision 10), and a second outcome on it is `409 safety_outcome_not_allowed`, as is any outcome on an event not in `safety_review`.
19. **Every outcome is a `safety_outcome_recorded` history entry** (AC7): the officer as actor, the time, and `details` of `outcome`, `checklist` and `reason` (`null` on an approval without one), saved in the same commit as the status. It is internal-only, like the equipment outcome (ADR-0040): the organiser sees the status change, not the checklist.
20. **The assigned coordinator is told** (AC8) with one of three new notification kinds, `safety_check_approved`, `safety_check_rejected` and `safety_changes_requested`, and the reason as the `detail`, after the commit (ADR-0015). Nobody else is told. Migration `0032_add_safety_outcome` adds the history kind and the three notification kinds outside the transaction; its downgrade deletes those rows and rebuilds both types. An event an outcome sent back to planning stays `approved`.
21. **Defaults to review (not agreed with the team), SCRUM-98:** decision 16 (every note required, even on Reject), decision 17's "planning is `approved`" and Approve keeping `safety_review`, decision 18's "a fresh submission voids an approval", and decision 19's internal-only history entry.

## Still owed
- **Frontend:** a Safety Officer's page for the list, and the new notification kind's message and link. The backend is ready.
- **ADR-0040's deferred AC6:** a revised equipment outcome after the check forces a new one. SCRUM-98 does not do this.
- **SCRUM-99:** flagging the arrangements the Safety Officer names, showing the reasons beside them, re-entering Safety Review once they are resolved, and telling Venue Staff and Technical Support. SCRUM-98 only returns the event to `approved`, from where the coordinator can already send it again.
- **SCRUM-49:** done by [ADR-0051](0051-confirm-an-event.md): the coordinator confirms from `safety_review` once `has_safety_approval` is true, and venue and equipment are re-checked then, which settles the open item below for confirmation.
- **SCRUM-84/85:** refusing preparation details until `has_safety_approval` is true.
- **Frontend:** the Safety Officer's outcome form, and the three new notification kinds' messages.
- **Open:** an event in Safety Review can't be edited, nor its equipment changed, because `safety_review` is in neither `EDITABLE_STATUSES` nor `EQUIPMENT_EDITABLE_STATUSES`. SCRUM-98's Request Changes is the way back to editing. Venue arrangements are not blocked yet: the coordinator could still withdraw or change one during review, or raise a new one, which the list would then show beside the approved ones without its status. SCRUM-98 or SCRUM-49 should decide.

## Consequences
- SCRUM-98 and SCRUM-49 have the status, the role and the gate to build on.
- The frontend knows the new role and status (chip label "Safety review"); a Safety Officer lands on the staff home until the frontend gives them a page for the list.

- The team settles decisions 3, 6 or 8.
- On Hold (SCRUM-75) or Cancelled arrangement statuses land: check they count as active or retired as AC1 says.
