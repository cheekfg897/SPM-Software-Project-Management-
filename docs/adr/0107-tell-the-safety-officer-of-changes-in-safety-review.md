# ADR-0107: A significant change in Safety Review tells every Safety Officer, and sends the event back to Approved when it unsettles an arrangement

- **Status:** Accepted; telling the Safety Officer and sending the event back to Approved are SCRUM-33 AC9 (Product Owner, 25 Oct); telling every Safety Officer, counting a line change as a significant change here, reading a flagged arrangement as "not Approved", judging only what the change itself did, naming every changed field, the wording, and writing no history entry for the move are defaults to review
- **Date:** 2026-10-26
- **Story:** SCRUM-33 Update event information and flag significant changes (AC9); SCRUM-37 AC4 (a line change is a significant change)
- **Supersedes:** [ADR-0101](0101-direct-edits-stop-at-confirmed.md) decision 6's default (an edit in Safety Review before the Safety Officer rules leaves the event there, untold); [ADR-0092](0092-equipment-line-changes-reopen-the-review.md) decision 1's "In Safety Review" bullet (a line change before the Safety Officer's outcome leaves the event in Safety Review); [ADR-0094](0094-reassess-each-venue-arrangement-after-a-significant-change.md) decision 11's "If not, it stays in Safety Review"
- **Extends:** [ADR-0087](0087-withdrawn-safety-approval-returns-the-event-to-approved.md), [ADR-0047](0047-safety-officer-and-safety-review.md), [ADR-0015](0015-in-app-notifications.md)

## Context
SCRUM-33 AC9, added on 25 Oct: "Given the event is in Safety Review, When a significant change is made, Then the Safety Officer is notified, naming the event and the fields that changed. If the change leaves an equipment line without a current outcome or a venue arrangement that is not Approved, the event returns to Approved (the planning stage) and re-enters Safety Review only once it meets SCRUM-97 again." The story's "Safety Review change clarification" gives the reason: an event enters Safety Review only once every venue arrangement is Approved and every equipment line has an outcome (SCRUM-97). "If a change undoes either of those while the event is already in Safety Review, it goes back to Approved rather than being reviewed against arrangements that are not settled."

What `main` did in Safety Review:

- **An edit (ADR-0101 decision 6).** A significant change withdrew an approval the Safety Officer had given, which sent the event back to Approved (AC7, ADR-0087). With no approval yet, the event stayed in Safety Review and nobody was told. Any arrangement the change affected was flagged (ADR-0094), but stayed Approved.
- **A line change (ADR-0092).** Adding, changing or removing a line is a significant change (SCRUM-37 AC4). An added or changed line has no outcome until Technical Support review it again. The event stayed in Safety Review unless an approval was withdrawn.

## Decision
1. **Who is told: every Safety Officer,** once per change, with a new `safety_review_event_changed` notification kind (migration 0046). Safety Officers aren't assigned to events, and SCRUM-97 AC4 already tells every one of them when an event enters Safety Review; both notices now go through one helper, `_notify_safety_officers`. Nobody else hears of it: the coordinator made the change, and Technical Support and Venue Staff keep their own notices (ADR-0025, ADR-0092, ADR-0094). **Default to review:** every Safety Officer, not only one who has started the review, since the system doesn't record who has.
2. **When.** The event was in Safety Review when the change was made, and the change was significant:
   - **an edit** that changed at least one significant field (SCRUM-33 AC2). Notes alone, or significant values sent again unchanged, tell nobody;
   - **a line** added, given a new type or quantity, or removed. **Default to review:** AC9 says "a significant change", and SCRUM-37 AC4 calls each of these one. AC9's own words, "leaves an equipment line without a current outcome", describe something only a line change can do, since an edit never touches a line's outcome. New technical notes alone tell nobody.
3. **What it says.** The kind's message is "An event in Safety Review changed significantly." The notice names the event, through its own `event_name`, and links to it, as every notice does (ADR-0015). Its `detail` names the fields: "Changed: end time, expected attendance." A field is named by its API name with spaces ("proposed date"), except `internal_planning_notes`, which is "planning notes", as SCRUM-33 AC1 has called them since 25 Oct; a line change is "equipment line", the field ADR-0092 records it under. If the event went back to Approved, it adds "It is back in planning until it can be sent to Safety Review again." **Default to review:** every field the edit changed is named, notes too, and not only the significant ones. A note changed beside a date change is still part of what the officer is now reviewing.
4. **Back to Approved.** **Default to review: only what the change itself did counts.** The clarification's "a change undoes either of those" points this way, and so does "the change leaves":
   - **A line added, or given a new type or quantity,** has no current outcome (ADR-0092 decision 4), so the event goes back.
   - **A removed line** leaves no line without an outcome, so the event stays.
   - **An edit** never changes an arrangement's status. What it can do is flag one for Venue Staff to reconfirm, or as no longer suitable (ADR-0094). **Default to review:** a flagged arrangement counts as "not Approved". SCRUM-97's gate refuses it as it refuses a Pending one (ADR-0094 decision 9), and Venue Staff must look at it again. So an edit that flags an arrangement sends the event back: a change of date or time, or of the venue requirements, flags every live arrangement, and a rise in attendance flags those it outgrows. One that flags nothing (a fall in attendance, or new equipment requirements) leaves the event in Safety Review, and the officer goes on with it as it now stands.
5. **Moved once, in the change's own commit.** `withdraw_safety_approval` runs first, as before. If it already sent the event back (the officer had approved it), `_return_to_approved` finds it Approved and leaves it alone. Only one status change and one `safety_approval_withdrawn` entry are written, and each officer gets one notice. A change that fails to save, such as a duplicate line (`409`), moves nothing and tells nobody. The notice goes out after the commit; one that can't be saved is logged and loses only itself (ADR-0015).
6. **Re-entering Safety Review** needs nothing new. The coordinator sends the event again, and `submit_for_safety_review` refuses it, naming each gap, until the flag clears or the line has its outcome (SCRUM-97, ADR-0094 decision 9).
7. **Default to review: no history entry of its own for the move.** The change's own `field_changed` entries say what changed and who changed it, and the flags say what happened to each arrangement (ADR-0094 decision 12). An event the officer had approved also gets its `safety_approval_withdrawn` entry. A new history kind would need a migration of `event_history_entry_kind` for a step those entries already explain.
8. **No change for an event in any other status.** An Approved event stays Approved, and only an edit or a line change made while the event is in Safety Review tells the Safety Officer.

### The web app and the mock
9. **The web app** needs no new screen. After an edit, the page reads the event again (ADR-0101 decision 9), so it shows Approved when the change sent the event back. The Safety Officer reads the notice in their notifications, which already show every kind's message and detail.
10. **The mock** (`eventEdits.ts`) sends an event in Safety Review back to Approved when the edit flags one of its planned arrangements, after any withdrawal, so it moves once. The notifications mock serves only seeded notices, so it raises none (as ADR-0103 decision 5). **Not mirrored:** the equipment-line mock keeps its own events, apart from the records the pages read, and shows no status. A line change there can't move a status anything reads. Mirroring it waits for the mocks to share one store of events (a ticket to open, beside ADR-0092's for reservations).

## Consequences
- The Safety Officer hears of every significant change while reviewing, and knows when the event left their queue and why.
- An event in Safety Review that a change unsettles leaves the officer's list at once. The officer no longer reviews against arrangements Venue Staff or Technical Support must look at again.
- One edit can send three kinds of notice: Technical Support's, Venue Staff's (one per flagged arrangement) and the Safety Officers'.
- Tests: `tests/events/test_safety_review_changes.py` and `tests/events/test_safety_review_event_changed_migration.py` (marker `scrum_33`); two older tests that expected the event to stay in Safety Review after a line change now set up their data around the new rule (`test_equipment_line_reopens_review.py`, `test_submit_for_safety_review.py`); the mock's `eventEdits.test.ts`; `EditEventPage.test.tsx`; `planning.int.test.ts` against the backend.

## Revisit when
- The team reads "a venue arrangement that is not Approved" as its status alone, so a flagged arrangement doesn't send the event back (decision 4).
- The team wants any unsettled arrangement, not only one this change unsettled, to send the event back (decision 4).
- A Safety Officer is assigned to an event: tell only them (decision 1).
